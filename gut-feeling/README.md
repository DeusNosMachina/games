# Operation: Gut Feeling

A hospital adventure for Macy: volleyball and softball phenom, gremlin wrangler, and reluctant future returner to school.

**[Play on phone or laptop](https://deusnosmachina.github.io/games/gut-feeling/)** · [Games catalog](../README.md)

## v0.7: the gameplay pass

The first live playtest said the arcades swung between too easy and too hard, with Bedside Blaster the one genuine arcade game. So every other arcade was rebuilt or reworked against that bar, and every one now ships with a human-speed yardstick (`tests/tools/`) that must clear level 1 nearly always, level 2 most of the time and level 3 sometimes at a quarter-second reaction. Drip or Die is no longer an arcade: her room has Fastpitch Fever by day, and the IV is only the recovery, one round at the level of the game that flattened her, never a dead end. The MRI has pinball, the CT has a real race, the lounge has a paddle breakout, Gel Yeah! is pure slicing from the first swipe, and the ward plays landscape with a joystick and a big SWING button, a combo slam, snack carts and a paced wave 3.

Six arcade games with three levels each, a ward that fights back properly, a boss named Ralph, music, fullscreen play, and a failure animation that finally earns its name. Level 1 of everything teaches you. Levels 2 and 3 are the real deal.

**Drip or Die** is no longer an arcade. It is the recovery: zero hearts puts Macy in bed for one round, at the level of the game that flattened her (Legendary included), and a coin flip picks which: prime the pump (tap when the line crosses mint) or hold the pressure (hold to pump, keep the needle in a band that jumps while she coughs and hiccups). Three strikes is still the staged cartoon explosion. Pop and you can try again (same round, same level) or get patched up for 15% of your coins; either way you are back in the ward with a heart. No run is a dead end.

Every game shows its full briefing once. After that you get a 1.2-second GO card with the one-line rule and a 1.5-second countdown. Reduced-motion settings keep the longer reading time.

The game opens on a proper title screen: the hospital at dusk, gremlin eyes in the dark windows, one START. Any key or any tap starts it, and a saved game picks up where you left off. The pause menu has Quit to title.

Each arcade is the canvas plus the minimum: three pills for progress, time and lives along the canvas edge, the instructions as a short toast, and the controls: one row under the canvas (or a column beside it) with a mouse; on phones and tablets the game fills the screen and the buttons float over its bottom edge, and pinball puts LEFT and RIGHT in the corners with BALL SAVE between them. The lobby is one screen: the levels as a row of four, a Steady hands / Show-off switch, START and BACK TO THE HOSPITAL, with the rules behind a How to play tap.

The map is a drawn floor plan of the seventh floor of Valley Hospital in Paramus (Pediatrics, Room 10): every room names its arcade with its medal dots, tap a room to go there, tap a game's name or PLAY to start its arcade, LIGHTS OUT in your room for the night shift (Bedside Blaster), and the Daily Dose, gift shop and exit (which tells you what it still wants) sit where you would expect them. The restroom is occupied. Knock anyway. Walk the hallway between rooms and talk to the visitors (on a phone a tap only opens a talk when the TALK prompt is up over that visitor; every other tap walks): Dad (a fresh dad joke, or about one time in three a deep thought from Kant, Jung, Spinoza, Plato or Sagan landed with a one-liner), Riley, Mimi and Dodo (silver side part, beard and a camera; a bun, a monogram purse and a lot of worry about whether she has eaten), Presley, Norah and Taylor, cousin Casey, nurses Stephanie and Julez, Dr. Lori and Dr. Benedetto, Barb from Dietary with her Ensure, and Rowdy the Ram at the nurses' station. Every visitor has a deck of ten or more conversations that remembers where it left off across visits and reloads. Nobody comes to her but Barb: visitors stay in their spots and turn to her, stroll only after a minute or two idle, and Rowdy runs the length of the hall screaming a cheer every couple of minutes. Visitors also roam: doctors and nurses may be in any room but the family lounge, family and friends in her room, the lounge or the hallway, Barb anywhere, Rowdy only in the hallway, nobody in a ward that still has gremlins, and never more than three in a room (Mimi and Dodo count as one, so do the three friends). Someone moves every 20 to 60 seconds, and if she is in either room she sees them walk out one door and in the other. Stand next to someone for a moment and you get a greeting: a hug from Dad, a fist bump (or an arm flick) from Riley, a selfie with the three friends, a group hug with Mimi and Dodo, a high five from Casey. Every visit pins a card to the get-well wall above her bed.

## The ward fights back

Four departments have gremlins. Each has three waves, and the first wave of your first visit is a teaching wave with hints. Clear a ward once to unlock its arcade. A cleared room stays cleared, through the map, the hallway, an arcade, a pause and a reload, until you press REMATCH (two presses in the ward strip, one in the pause menu); then Ralph's grudge rises: more gremlins, faster, bigger payout. A fight you leave mid-wave remembers who was still standing.

Each department has its own twist, taught once. The MRI's magnet pulls every 13 seconds after a warning: bruisers fly into the bore by their helmets and three loose bedpans bowl whatever they cross (kick one into line). The lounge has a remote on the couch: hit it and the TV flips to the cat cam, and every gremlin in front of the screen zones out for a few seconds. The CT's Donut II rolls through the lit lane every 12 seconds and bowls gremlins and Macy alike unless she jumps or shields. The ultrasound floor has gel puddles that make everyone slide, knocks skid into walls, and charges overshoot.

Hitting a gremlin staggers it. Stuns come only from the third hit of a combo, the spin, a brick or a stomp, so mashing one button no longer works. Hit a gremlin twice in a row and it hops out of reach, angry.

Three landed attacks in a row (each within four seconds, no whiff, no hit taken) charge a COMBO: a gold ring fills around the attack button and three pips show over Macy. The next attack with a gremlin in reach is a slam that hits everything within 150 px, knocks it back, stuns it for 0.9 s and cancels its wind-up. A slam pays five coins; the first one each room drops a nutrient, then every third.

First clears are paced: waves 2 and 3 keep at most three gremlins up at once (four on Absolute Gutastrophe), the rest come out one at a time, and spit, charge and lob wind-ups run a quarter longer. Rematches have no cap and no longer tells.

- **Nibblers** hunt in pairs, flank, crouch and lunge.
- **Spitters** strafe, lead their shots, back off when you close in, and spread-shoot on wave 3. A pillow swing sends blobs back at them.
- **Bruisers** charge. A charge into furniture or a wall leaves them dizzy, which is when you hit them. From the front they shrug off pillow pokes.
- **Split peas** split on the first hit and re-merge if you leave the halves alone for four seconds.
- **Hurlers** lob gunk bricks that leave sticky puddles. They show up from wave 2 in later rooms.

| Action | What it does | Supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SWING | The pillow: a three-hit combo, deflects blobs; hold to keep swinging, and the third swing of a held run spins | always |
| JUMP | Over low attacks; swing in the air to stomp on landing | always |
| DASH | Double-tap the joystick (or Q): through a gremlin mid-wind-up to cancel its attack | always |
| SHIELD | The barf-bag bubble, up while held: nothing gets through, and she cannot swing from inside; a 2.5 s meter that refills | always |
| BRICK | A lob that auto-leads the nearest gremlin and lands a stunning burst | 3 per fight, plus one glinting on the floor each wave to pick up (carry 5) |

**Ralph** waits in your room once your passport is complete. Phase 1 is a spit barrage you deflect back at him. Phase 2 is charges that leave him dizzy when he hits the bed, plus minions. Phase 3 is leaping slams that shake the room. Beat him to get discharged.

## Phone, tablet and desktop controls

On phones and tablets the joystick scheme is the default: a joystick bottom left (it wears a »: double-tap it in any direction to dash) with EAT just to its right, and an action cluster bottom right: SWING in the corner, JUMP beside it, SHIELD above SWING and BRICK between them. In landscape the room fills the screen and the controls float over it; in portrait they sit under the room, and a phone held upright gets a one-time "turn your phone sideways" pill. "Tap the room" (the floor is the controller) is one switch away in the pause menu or the settings, and a chosen scheme is kept.

| Action | Joystick scheme | Tap the room | Keyboard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move | Left joystick | Tap the floor; drag to steer | WASD / arrows |
| Swing | SWING; hold to repeat, drag to aim | Tap a gremlin (hold to keep swinging) | Space |
| Jump | JUMP | Two-finger tap, or JUMP | Shift / J |
| Dash | Double-tap the joystick | Flick | Q |
| Shield | Hold SHIELD | Long-press the floor, or hold SHIELD | Hold R |
| Brick | BRICK (shows how many you carry) | BRICK | B |
| Nutrient | EAT (shows how many you carry) | EAT | F |
| Map | Map button | Map button | M |
| Nearby arcade | Play button, or tap the beacon | Same | Enter |
| Pause | Pause button | Pause button | Escape |

In her room and the hallway, where there are no gremlins, the cluster slims to the joystick, EAT and one big button that reads USE or TALK. Every touch button acts the moment your finger lands, so JUMP and BRICK work with a thumb on the joystick, and SHIELD tracks its own finger. SWING is 96 to 128 px depending on the screen; a drag on it aims.

Fullscreen ("arcade mode") pins the game to the screen, and phones and tablets enter it when you press START. iPad Safari uses the real fullscreen API; iPhones use a CSS fallback because Safari has none for pages. Tablets get bigger buttons, and the lobby and results use the width.

Arcade controls:

- **Fastpitch:** swipe up through it and let go; harder goes farther, a longer swipe throws higher; arrows aim and set power, W/S the arc, Space throws; THROW repeats the last flick.
- **Recovery (Drip or Die):** on the pump round, tap anywhere or Space when the line crosses mint; on the pressure round, hold anywhere or Space to pump and let go to ease off.
- **Pinball:** left half or Z / Left flips left, right half or slash / Right flips right, hold both for the magnet, tap or Space to launch, swipe up or Shift to nudge (three quick nudges tilt).
- **Bricks:** slide a finger (or the mouse) to move the tray; press to serve; arrows or A/D slide, Space serves, Up or W fires a pillow.
- **Race:** hold left or right (arrows, A/D) to steer, drag to steer gently, swipe up or Shift to boost, tap a gurney or Space to throw.
- **Gel:** press and swipe; a press on a gremlin slices it at once. Bombs only count on a moving swipe through their middle; purple gas fogs the screen instead of costing a heart. E freezes the gel for five seconds.
- **Blaster:** tap to shoot, or slide and let go; tap an edge arrow or slide into the edge to look; hold and release to pierce; STICK (by the charges) shows a thumbstick, remembered. Mouse: the sight follows the pointer, click fires, right-drag looks. R reloads; tap R again in the green for an instant reload.
- **All games:** E uses a limited assist; Escape pauses.

## Progression and difficulty

**Steady hands** gives two assists per run. **Show-off shift** removes them and pays 25% more. The prescriptions are **Pillow Fort**, **Questionable Decisions** and **Absolute Gutastrophe**; they change speed, counts, windows and payouts. Every arcade has a fourth level, **Legendary**, unlocked by any medal on level 3: a single fixed test tuned so a quarter-second player wins about one time in four, which always runs on Questionable Decisions rules whatever prescription is set.

Medals are bronze, silver and gold, worth 1, 2 and 3 discharge stars, on every level including Legendary. Discharge needs 21 stars, a medal in all six games, any level 2 medal, and Ralph beaten; the exit and the map list exactly what is still missing.

- **Daily Dose:** the date picks a game and a level. Same run for everyone, local best per day, a streak, and a bonus the first time each day.
- **Gift shop:** eight permanent upgrades (grippy socks, pillow reach, longer shield, thick skin, pocket snacks, Ralph repellent, arcade pass, a lucky scrunchie for a sixth heart) and three wardrobe looks for Macy.
- **Hall of Nonsense:** gremlins evicted, best chain, ward clears, lifetime coins, runs played, daily streak.

Nutrients restore one heart and go in the pocket (three, or five with Pocket snacks). Every room has a snack cart (the lounge has its SNAX machine) that hands out one nutrient and restocks after a ward clear there or two minutes of ward time; a first clear of wave 1 or 2 drops one; every sixth eviction drops one; combo slams drop them too. Floor drops last 30 seconds and leave with you. Zero hearts sends Macy to one gentle IV phase; failing it is where the organs come out, and the nurses patch her up for a few coins either way. All medical mechanics are fictional arcade jokes.

## Her room, and the rest of the hospital

A light switch on the wall between the get-well board and the monitor turns her room into the night room and back (Bedside Blaster lives there). Macy is 5'10", wears 22, and stands around when you leave her alone: she looks about, fixes her scrunchie, bounces a volleyball. Her room has the LEGO water lilies on the sill, the LEGO Jaguar E-Type on the cabinet, a window on Paramus that follows the real clock, and a 75-inch TV with a cat cam (Addie asleep, Stella grooming, Pepper attacking the hand that feeds her), a Rams game, a get-well slideshow and, after dark, the 3 AM channel that should not exist. Every machine on the floor has opinions and one of them is Employee of the Month each day.

Refuse Barb's Ensure often enough and the hospital fills with it: bottles in rows, a storage closet on the map you are not to ask about, gremlins carrying bottles as shields, a cafeteria renamed The Ensure Experience, and finally one bottle that reads FINE. YOU WIN. on a pedestal in her room.

Stand at the foot of her bed and NAP: the lights go out and Macy dreams on the spot, a 40-second interlude from a pool of 25 vignettes with its own woozy theme, until a nurse wakes her (lying still in the dark for a while drifts off too). Dream logic runs all through it: a hallway that stretches the faster she walks, a door that opens onto the same room five deep, a sideways elevator, teeth that turn into Tic Tacs, Pepper the size of a sofa, her number drifting 22, 23, 2, a false wake-up that turns out to still be the dream, and the psychology jokes for whoever is watching over her shoulder (a Rorschach gremlin, Jung's shadow waving, a Freudian slip, Pavlov's bell before the snack cart, Maslow's pyramid of pudding, the Stroop sign, a melting Dali clock, Escher stairs, and a dream analysis card: she misses her cats). Visitors leave her room when the lights are out.

Medals land with a stamp, confetti and a sting (Rowdy gallops across on gold). There is a sheet of 36 stickers to earn, and a Share button that renders a card as a PNG for the family chat.

## Sound

Everything is synthesized in the browser: a music track per room and per game, a boss theme, win and lose stings, and about fifty sound effects. Sound is on by default and the button in the header or the pause menu turns it off. The preference is saved.

## Build and verify

No package installation, runtime dependencies, remote assets, or backend.

```sh
node gut-feeling/tests/run-all.cjs
```

That builds `index.html` from `src/` and runs every test file. `python3 gut-feeling/build.py` builds on its own. Run from the repository root and commit `src/` and the generated `index.html` together.

Tests: `verify.cjs` (saves, run lifecycle, idle failure in all 54 combinations, discharge, the title screen, touch controls, run-screen sizing, the hallway hooks), `map.cjs` (the floor plan), `combat.cjs` (ward rules, Ralph, and a check that a button-masher dies while a dodging bot wins), `audio.cjs`, `smoke.cjs`, one `game-<name>.cjs` per arcade, and `playthrough.cjs`, which drives an input-only bot from `tests/bots/` through every game, level and prescription. They establish rules and solvability. Human playtesting still decides whether the quotas feel right.

The code layout, the game registry and the rules for editing are in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Design references

The implementations, characters, and artwork are specific to this game. Familiar mobile ideas informed it:

- [Halfbrick's Fruit Ninja guide](https://www.halfbrick.com/blog/the-ultimate-beginners-guide-to-fruit-ninja): deliberate multi-target swipes, combos, and power-up windows.
- [Halfbrick's Jetpack Joyride](https://www.halfbrick.com/games/jetpack-joyride-classic): continuous arcade runs and changing obstacles.
- [Supercell's Brawl Stars](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/): compact combat sessions with varied attacks and modes.

## Saves and hosting

Progress lives in localStorage for the current browser and origin. It does not sync between devices. Saves from v0.5 carry over; v0.6 adds the new counters, the daily record, the shop items and the Ralph flags. A fresh-adventure reset clears progress only after a second deliberate click.

GitHub Pages serves `main` from the repository root. The game also works as a single local HTML file; the icons (`favicon.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`), the link preview card (`og.png`, the title screen) and `manifest.webmanifest` sit beside it so a shared link shows a card and "Add to Home Screen" gets a gremlin. No account, analytics, cloud save, or service worker.
